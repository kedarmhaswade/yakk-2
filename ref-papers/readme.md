# Outlook
One should pay attention to papers in their field of interest. Researchers are human beings, and they have ulterior motives to write papers accordingly. But if you trust the contribution of certain researchers, you should assess it by only reading their papers.

Research papers, at least those that become famous, are usually freely available on the Internet. Just because a paper is famous doesn't mean it is a contribution to our understanding, however. 

How do I choose AI papers to read then? Once something becomes a part of human trust, intuition etc., rationality takes a lower precedence (rightfully so, because when time is finite, and meaningful reading of research papers is extremely time-consuming), and one must take a leap of faith. If the reading of a paper so chosen from a sea of preprint or published papers brings joy, illuminates something (even amid some confusion), I'd say that the endeavor had been worth it.
